### Science

Inside LLMs there are many layers. These layers adds to the initial input vectors which came from the embedded tokens. What we are looking at is the residual stream which is a constant iteration of the vectors (what's the specific name). As such, it serves as a memory lane.

What we are investigating is whether the layers inside the LLM "thinks" in English. As such, we are giving a translation task like Japanese to German and then using a tool called "logit lens" to peek into the residual stream to see what it is "thinking about" at every layer. We have also tested language models with repetition tasks like Japanese to Japanese which involves no translations at all. These results manage to produce rather interesting results using Gemma 2 2b. The translation and repetition tasks both produced peaks of the English versions of the words. Logit lens, together with some mathematical functions, help to produce a probability distribution of all the words it is thinking about. Thus, peaks referring to huge probabilities.

Another interesting point that is worth highlighting is that between the translation and repetition tasks, the translation task sustained a high probability for English versions of the word for a period of time, while the peak observed in the repetition task lasted for much fewer layers. On top of that, the experiment conducted in this repo uses a different model from the paper that we are currently referencing.

Yes, for now, all the experiments have been a reproduction of the results already displayed in a paper. However, one useful note that we are testing is whether there's a correlation between first token availability and height of the peak. Height of the peak refers to the max probability throughout the forward pass. First token availability (FTA) refers to whether the words is requires one or more tokens to represent it. For translation tasks, they suggested that languages with a higher percentage of words being completely FTA such as Mandarin, will observe a lower English peak while languages like Russian will observe a higher English peak. The suggested reason is that for single-token words allow the model to immediately answer the question without having to keep hold of the knowledge inside "its memory". For multi-token answers, the model will need to hold onto that knowledge for longer as it will be needed for the next token as well. From prior experiments, it is observed that this might not be the case. However, the experiments have bugs in them which needs fixing.
